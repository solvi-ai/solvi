# Roadmap

What we plan to add or improve, roughly in priority order. Research items ship only if their pre-registered measurements
hold; the numbers are published either way (see the research notes linked from each release). Ideas and votes are welcome
in [Discussions → Ideas](https://github.com/solvi-ai/solvi/discussions/categories/ideas).

Status: `planned` · `in progress` · `research` (may not ship) · `done (version)`.

## Toward 1.0: what solvi is for, and everything that exists landed

First the goal and an honest account of what is there; the module cleanup and the split into two levels come after,
and follow from it.

| Item | What it gives | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **The goal and the mission, stated** | [docs/mission.md](docs/mission.md). solvi builds decision systems from fast solvers (rules, code, small models) and LLMs: the fast ones answer where they are sure, LLMs and search deliberate where they are not, a person decides what neither can; every decision is recorded, explainable and replayable. **Accountability and safety:** see how each model solves each task — what it read, who answered, what was promised, whether the promise held — and wrap any model in checks that keep it from doing harm. **Accumulated knowledge:** the system keeps verified knowledge about its environment — rules, action conditions, a world map, goals (an agenda with code done checks and gates); a written specification is compiled into rules, otherwise rules are learned from experience, each with its source, and every item can be checked and retracted. Knowledge protects by default (with its source and exact retraction); justified risk is an option that lowers the cost of protection without promising parity. Honest limits: growth was shown only in environments met again (a crafting game, the Pokémon world map), not on streams of one kind of decision (classification, matching) or for a support agent with tools, and the promise does not hold between an abrupt shift and its detection. **Any model**, including a small local one so much runs without the cloud (no quality promise for it). **Where to use:** single decisions (tickets, documents and contracts, policy decisions) and sequential decisions in an environment (agents with tools, planning, games). **What it is not:** a text generator, a hosted service or a replacement for LLMs; where a guarantee cannot be given it hands over to a person. **Honesty:** only what measurably helps enters the library, negative results are published next to positive ones, and a promise is a number you can check. | done |
| **Frequent decisions: a compact journal, budgets, outcomes as labels** | From a field report on 14 test setups (a game loop wrote 14 KB a decision, 1.4 GB in an evening; model spend had no limit; outcomes the agent saw were not labels): compact records (`record="compact"` / `"sample:N"`, 2.0–5.2× smaller, still verified and replayed, what cannot be checked said as `not_kept`) and rotation of a JSONL store with linked segments; `Budget` / `Cost` on `solvi.core.slow.generate` and `solvi.core.slow.refine` (tokens, dollars, latency; stop by total or per decision; in the trace and the system report); `System.outcome` stores an outcome as a label for explicit recalibration; on-the-fly recalibration only in the experimental `solvi.experimental.oncalib`; full references of the refine loop and of `systemone`. | done (1.0) |
| **Everything that exists, landed** | Each module and feature in one of three places: works, with the measured value and the script behind the number; experimental, with what is missing; or removed. The README, the guide and the API reference follow that split. Removals done (CHANGELOG 1.0.0 "Removed"); `memory` and `episode` folded into the knowledge memory, `extract_multi` removed. | done (1.0) |

### Then, in 1.0: two levels and an experimental module

Built on the account above: what works becomes the stable low level, and the high level is put together from it.

| Item | What it gives | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Low level: building blocks with stable extension points | Runtime, parts, questions, storage and traces, guards, deciders, slow paths and dispatch as documented base classes and protocols you can subclass or replace, with a promise of which ones stay stable; your own pieces keep tracing, replay and guarantees; `solvi.core` exports every extension point, `solvi.testing.conformance` checks yours ([Building blocks](docs/building_blocks.md)) | done (1.0) |
| High level: ready-made systems | A System 1 + System 2 system in one, where you choose the models and providers and state what you need (questions, examples, risk, budget); built only from the low level, so any piece can be swapped out. `solvi.build` and `solvi.Guard`; `solvi.Knowledge` (store, agenda, action model, failure memory in one object; `build(knowledge=)`, `Guard(knowledge=)`) and `solvi.Agent` (an environment agent: System 1 on what the knowledge predicts, System 2 a search, hard checks from gates and refusals, protection by default and `RiskBudget` as an option, every decision replayable; example 25). Next: the agent on a real game environment with a measured run before any claim beyond the toy | in progress (on `dev`) |
| Clear split in docs and imports | A short path for the high level; a reference for the low level: the docs in three parts (Using solvi, Building blocks, Experimental), the API reference by level, `solvi migrate` for the new import paths | done (1.0) |
| `solvi.experimental` | Pieces that work but are not yet measured well enough for the stable promise live in one submodule, with what they still need to graduate (`solvi.experimental.STATUS`, [docs/experimental.md](docs/experimental.md)); graduate or go by 1.2 | done (1.0) |

## Next: streaming guarantees and a learning loop

Today a promise is calibrated once on a labelled set and then watched for drift. These items earn and keep the promise on
the live stream instead, and let a model learn from the labels the stream produces without losing the promise. The
streaming design adapts ideas from an Apache-2.0 project. The first six items are on `dev` as experimental
(`solvi.experimental.stream`, 1.4.0, unreleased) and were measured once; their limits are in
[docs/experimental.md](docs/experimental.md#stream), and no gain is claimed beyond that measurement. The identity
canary, the label check before calibration, a person's error rate and the known-answer tests for the guarantee
arithmetic are on `dev` too; their rows, with what was measured, are in the 1.2 table above.

| Item | What it gives | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **A streaming promise on a threshold grid** | `StreamPromise`: an anytime-valid learn-then-test certificate earned on the stream, one betting e-process per threshold on a grid fixed before any label; the threshold in force only loosens, and a restart after a change spends δ as 6δ/(π²v²) so the guarantee holds over every restart. Why: a promise that can be checked at any time, not only after a fixed calibration set. Next: the stand | measured once (experimental): no certificate above the promise on any measured stream (i.i.d. between change points, coin independent of the input, the detection-lag window exempted); below the offline certificate's coverage from a cold start; numbers in [best practices](docs/best_practices.md#guarantees-and-calibration) |
| **A risk detector on the deployed loss** | `RiskDetector`: a Shiryaev–Roberts e-detector on how often a decision answered alone was wrong; an alarm restarts the certificate and sends every decision to the slow path or a person for a probation period and until it is certified again. Why: the promise does not hold between a shift and its detection; this shortens that gap with a stated false-alarm rate. Next: the stand | measured once (experimental): its run-length statement holds in samples; far slower than the confidence CUSUM (`DriftMonitor` before its 1.4.0 window z-test change) on new-intent shifts and silent on corrupted text; keep it next to the score monitor |
| **Secret-keyed sampling for labels** | Which decisions get a label is decided by an HMAC coin over a secret and the decision number (not its input), at a rate set from the past only; a replay recomputes each coin. Why: calibrating only on escalated decisions loosens the threshold past the promise; a coin the input cannot steer does not. Next: the stand | measured once (experimental): the replay recomputed every coin exactly |
| **An agent as the label oracle, over MCP** | `TicketDesk` / `AgentOracle`: an outside agent labels sampled decisions through blind tickets (the question, the options and the input, never the model's answer); a ticket that expires goes to a person and counts as a loss. Why: labels from an agent without leaking the answer it should check, and no silent gain from missing labels. Next: the stand | experimental; unmeasured on a stream |
| **A learning loop with frozen versions** | `LearningLoop`: a live learner takes the labels, and frozen copies become candidates; each is certified only on samples drawn after its freeze, from one δ budget over all versions, and is promoted only above a floor of decisions it answers alone; rollback to any version. Why: a model that improves on the stream without the improvement eating the promise. Next: the stand measures whether it gains anything | measured once (experimental): no gain with gold labels (promotions by recall); the promise held for every served version; kept only as the core of the adapter loop. On `dev` (1.4.0): the promotion compares the candidate with the served version certified the same way on the same post-freeze samples (a comparison certificate at the candidate's δ_v that spends no δ); before, a fresh candidate lost on the served certificate's age (0 of 78 promoted in a LoRA stand, 53 under the new rule on the same data); a gain counts only on a new run; also on `dev`: additive coverage (`AdapterLoop(additive=True)`: a certified candidate serves the zone the served version escalates, a chain of up to `max_layers` layers, the guarantee per zone, stream-wide joint risk ≤ α·(1 + Σ P(Z_j))), the δ index taken at a promise's first sample, and a maturity gate on when a training starts; checked on synthetic streams, no stand yet |
| **Late labels in sampling order** | A label that arrives late is held until every earlier sampled decision has closed, so the certificate reads its samples in the order they were drawn. Why: labels that arrive out of order must not change what the promise says. Next: review | experimental; tested (simulated delays), not measured on a stream |
| **An estimate of the live error from few labels** | A PPI++ estimate: the few labelled decisions plus the model's scores on every decision, meant to give a narrower interval for the live error than the labels alone. Measured on a pre-registered stand: with 25–50 labels and rare errors its interval under-covered the true error (it collapsed to zero width when the sample drew no error), and with more labels it was barely narrower than the Wilson interval on the labels alone. The system report now gives that Wilson interval on the audited sample instead. | measured; not shipped |
| **A conformal test martingale** | A test that the stream is still exchangeable with calibration, as an alternative or complement to `DriftMonitor`, with a false-alarm guarantee at any stopping time. Measured on a pre-registered stand against `DriftMonitor`'s confidence CUSUM at the same false-alarm level: it held its level and caught every shift, but later than the CUSUM. | measured; not shipped |
| **A live-error interval valid at small label counts** | An interval for the live error from few labels plus the model's scores that stays valid when errors are rare: λ fixed in advance with an exact bound on the rectifier, or a variance floor. Bar, set before the stand: coverage ≥ 0.93 and width no wider than the Wilson interval at every label count up to 100. Why: the estimate above fails exactly where few labels are all there is. Measured on a pre-registered stand (three intent datasets, one decider, one run; the development stream was seen before the bars were fixed): λ fixed in advance with a betting bound was valid and 1.5–1.9× the Wilson width; a post-stratified Wilson interval on the act probability was valid in every cell, shifts included, but wider than Wilson at 25 and 50 labels among the answers given alone (the score explains 9–12 % of the error's variance there, and each stratum pays Wilson's small-sample cost again). The control that decides the claim: the same LLM with the same budget playing directly. Measured so far: growth across episodes in the crafting game and on the Pokémon map; on NetHack a carried memory cut the learnable deaths but lowered progress. Measured 2026-10 on text games under one frozen protocol (three free interactive-fiction games and 50 generated cooking games, 5,441 episodes): on the generated games the runtime scores six times the direct LLM at the same budget, but a rules-only player scores the same without the LLM, and a scrambled memory speeds later episodes as much as the real one; on the interactive-fiction games neither the rules nor the budgeted LLM moved the score, and the rules never asked the LLM where they knew nothing, while the direct LLM with 100 calls did score on one game. So "justified risk" leaves the claim too. Done since: the escalation rule now depends on a rule's coverage and track record and decays while the score stands still, and a rule acts on its own when no help is available. What remains is the direct-LLM control on the crafting game, not yet built. | research |
